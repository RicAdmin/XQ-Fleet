# XQ Car Blog Voice

## Editorial promise

Help a Langkawi traveller make a better rental, vehicle, route, timing, or cost decision. Deliver the useful answer before introducing XQ Car's commercial relevance. Be transparent when the operator is also the recommended solution.

## Register

Write like an experienced local operator explaining the practical choice across a counter: calm, exact, warm, and lightly opinionated when the evidence earns it. Prefer plain language, concrete trade-offs, and specific nouns. Keep promotional energy restrained; confidence comes from operational detail.

An XQ Car post should feel:

- answer-first rather than scene-setting
- locally informed rather than generically tropical
- commercially transparent rather than sales-shaped
- practical rather than exhaustive for its own sake
- current and dated where a fact can change

## Evidence boundary

Use “we” for facts supported by current project data or recorded XQ Car operator notes. Describe researched facts as researched facts and link the source when readers may act on them. Mark unconfirmed operational experience `operator check required` in the plan.

Use the repository as the authority for XQ Car claims:

- Brand identity and company relationship: `src/lib/brand.ts`, `src/components/about/AboutPage.tsx`, and current public message files.
- Fleet names, slugs, capacities, features, and configured prices: current database-facing code and `data/Car.csv` where it remains the active seed source.
- Booking, pricing, pickup, cancellation, payment, and policy behavior: current application code and legal modules. Treat planning documents as decisions or history, not proof of deployed behavior.
- Existing blog posts: overlap and internal-link evidence only. Re-verify their factual claims.

For island conditions outside XQ Car, prefer official operators, Malaysian government or tourism sources, primary documents, and named local reporting. Record the checked date for prices, schedules, rules, openings, and closures.

## XQ Car perspective

The distinctive contribution is operational judgement from serving self-drive visitors in Langkawi: matching people and luggage to vehicles, coordinating airport or jetty pickup, explaining booking trade-offs, and anticipating island-driving decisions. Use this perspective only where the evidence supports it.

Connect to XQ Car when it resolves the reader's next question. Name the trade-off as well as the benefit. A direct booking recommendation should follow a useful comparison or operational fact, not replace it.

## Language and structure

- Open with the answer or decision rule in a plain-text paragraph.
- Use descriptive H2s and H3s that remain meaningful out of context.
- State amounts in `RM` and give the effective or checked date when volatile.
- Use **XQ Car** in prose and **XQCar Team** only for the configured author name.
- Use Malaysian and British English conventions in English posts.
- Write Malay and Chinese posts as independent native-language articles with `-ms` and `-zh` slugs; retain proper brand and place names.
- End after the reader's decision is resolved. A concise booking invitation may follow when relevant.

## Voice risks

Replace brochure superlatives with observable detail. Replace generic adventure language with routes, timing, capacity, access, price, or process. Replace unsupported certainty with a dated source, a clear qualification, or an operator check. Keep keywords natural; one exact phrase in a title or opening is usually enough.

Generated cover art is an editorial illustration. Use a consistent XQ Car palette and composition that signals illustration rather than documentary proof of a real vehicle, location, or customer experience.
